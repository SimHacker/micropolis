• Simulation
    for Games

  • About

    This class explores the vast representational power of the computer. Computers can be used for so much more than simulating projectiles and manufactured landscapes—they are unlike any other medium in their ability to make playable complex systems about everything from ant colonies to cities, traffic jams, budgets, and geopolitics.

  • A spectrum of simulation approaches, from stock/flow systems to cellular automata, networks, physics, and agents are explored. Lectures introduce prototyping, visual design communication, and then go deep into the simulation topics by looking in detail at particular computer games, their design history, and source code (where possible), with a particular emphasis on SimCity. Relevant readings and game playing are assigned, and we make use of improv games to critique and sharpen our designs.

  • This is a studio based course. Every week students produced a one page design document and a working prototype employing the week‘s simulation topic. Final projects are prototypes of interactive explanations for Wikipedia. Students picked, researched and developed interactive simulations for Wikipedia pages.

    Stone Librande's one page design technique.

  • CMPS 179/1 was taught by Chaim Gingold in Spring of 2013, at UCSC. The syllabus contains detailed information about the schedule, activities, and readings.

    System Dynamics

  • About

  • Week 3

    Cellular Automata

  • About

    A key message of the class is that different simulation techniques imply different ways of thinking about and modeling the world. While system dynamics implies a high level of abstraction and non-spatiality, cellular automata (CA) conceptualize the world as spatial fields of massively parallel computation, where global dynamics arise from local interactions. I wrote a basic CA framework in Javascript, which was fun to do in a functional language. This became the basis for most of the student projects.

  • Week 6

    Design Redux

  • About

    This is the first week where we went off the syllabus in response to student feedback. While everyone kept up with the weekly design docs and prototypes, the schedule was a bit relentless, so we took a week to iterate design documents from previous weeks. While doing lots and lots of designs is one way to sharpen skills, iterating a single design seemed to have a nice effect, too. Two iterations were done on previous designs (which I had already given notes on). PDFs of the final version are linked to below.
