Mini game for SimCity: Street View Warrior

Backstory: 

To encourage competition and break up the giant digital mapping
monopolies of the 2010's, The Open Streetmap Mega-Corporation decreed
that they would license the most recent data gathered for each
geolocation on the map, until such a time as somebody else gathered
more recent data.

This lead the way to the great Street View Wars. 

You deploy a fleet of Google Street View cars around the map to gather
the most recent data, but your enemy has deployed combative TomTom
Street View cars around the city to foil your mission. 

It's a twisted metal cross between PacMan and Dungeon Keeper, where
you're driving around measuring GPS coordinates and taking panorama
pictures and trying to cover the whole city, intend of eating pills
and trying to clear the whole maze. 

The cars take 3d panoramas of each tile they drive on, to posess them.

It will be a fast action twitch combat game, so most the street view
photos will show pictures of explosions and combat during chase
scenes.

The cars can operate autonomously, driving around the map on their
own, and you can drag and drop them around like minions in dungeon
keeper, or take one of them first-person and drive it around.

They can fight each other and defend their territories. You can outfit
them with weapons and upgrades, and deploy specialized combat and
defenive vehicles. (The mapping cars are like the imps in dungeon
keeper, who cast the posession spells, but must be defended by other
stronger characters.)

If you're the most recent one to drive through a zone, you "posess" it
and earn licensing fees for the data during the time you data is the
most recent. 

The end result of the game is a KML overlay with each player's map,
including street view panorama from their point of view, rendered with
the lego brick houses of course. (or whatever virtual environment
you're playing in, even CG over real street view photos.)

Whoever took the last street view photo in a tile would "own" it, but
all previous ones would be available to flip through, to show what
happened on that tile.

So during a car chase, it record both driver's points of view, and you
can play them back together and see it from both perspectives.

So then you use the normal simcity editing tools to build interesting
maps for the game!
